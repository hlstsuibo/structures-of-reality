人類社會敘事史(2)：神權開啟的文明(上)——公平

神，無論是否存在，都以其超現實的能力與創世身份獲得了統治地位，在人類世界中擁有絕對正當性，成為人類歷史上極為特殊的至高權力階級。
​神，無論是否存在，人類對神的供奉與獻祭都在歷史上真實發生。即便在今天，飲食文化、婚姻倫理、死亡儀式、節日慶典中，神的身影依然無處不在。「神為主、人為僕、萬物皆為神之財物」的主從擁有關係，已深深植入人類文明，並伴隨社會演化延續至今。
​神如同人類理解世界最基礎的底層語言，與生活深度交融，滲透於一切意識形態之中。人類因神的能力、地位與創世身份而生出的那份特殊情感，如母親慈祥的目光，在集體協作與日常生活中流淌；現實中，動物界普遍存在的支配關係與領地佔有本能，在人類社會中具備廣泛的心理認可。
​因而，以神的權力地位為起點，將「神對萬物的擁有關係」樹立為人類社會關係的模板，建立起「跨本體擁有」秩序下的主從支配權力系統，並演化出人類彼此間的角色扮演與歸屬關係，便水到渠成。血統也因承襲了神的名望獲得正當性得而得到廣泛傳播，具備時代特徵的階級制度逐漸成形。
​神對萬物的「跨本體擁有」關係，就如同人類社會初始的座標系統，始終在確定著文明的進程與展開方向。

建立在擁有關係之上的勞動協作，在支配效率與組織穩定性上都優於鬆散的協同。在殘酷的自然選擇面前，鬆散的協作逐漸被基於擁有關係的支配性協作所取代。在生產力低下的時代，跨本體擁有主導下的主從關係與身份權力系統，實現了人盡其才、物盡其用。它幫助人類在自然選擇中取得優勢，並最終站到了世界舞台的中央。然而對於個人而言，這意味著只能被迫接受主從關係的安排，在既定的階級位置中填充與扮演自身的社會角色。這也導致後人常常誤以為，個人擁有「先天且生而具有的出身命運」。
​在人類文明進程中，跨本體擁有關係逐漸演變為穩定的、模型化的跨本體擁有權力。它透過符號宣稱與角色扮演，在與物理本體並無直接關聯的前提下，實現了社會世界對物理實體的佔有。它純粹是意識的宣稱，僅存在於人的意識之中，與人體擁有手腳這種物理上的本體性擁有，具有本質不同：擁有者與被擁有者之間的剝離，只會切斷情感寄託、導致意義消失，卻不會造成任何物理本體的傷害。然而，正是這種符號宣稱中的跨本體擁有權，其在意識中進行的邊界切割與意義賦予，構成了社會組織的基本骨架。
例如「這錢是我的」，「錢」本身並不屬於「我」的物理本體，「我」也不能像支配手腳那樣支配「錢」，但「錢」在社會關係中被認知為歸屬於「我」這一符號所代表的實體；「我」真正使用的，是「錢」這一符號在社會中的意義及其所代表的地位、資源與人際網絡。「擁有錢」不只是對物品的佔有，更是在確認一個人的社會地位、人際關係與可調用資源；再如「這片土地是中國的」，即「中國」這一符號，透過意識認知中的邊界切割與賦予意義，取得了對真實土地的排他性支配權；「這是一個美國人」——這個人在宣稱中屬於美國，被美國這一符號強制保護、支配，並排斥他國符號的調用。這一切正是現實社會的根本結構關係——跨本體擁有權為核心的社會結構。

物理世界中，萬物之間存在並列、交叉、包含、排斥等各種關係，但這些關係都不構成主從支配關係。即便是包含中有些關係會被稱為從屬，但其本質只是結構上的「屬於／包含於」，並不存在意志上的從屬——支配與服從。也就是說，萬物之間並不存在天然的主從支配，而是在各自獨立中自主運行。這種彼此互不從屬、互不支配、互不擁有、各自獨立的自主運行狀態，稱為平權，即萬物平權。萬物平權是天然的物理狀態，也是一種天然的平等關係。
人類與其擁有物之間，是由意識虛擬而生出的擁有關係，無法在物理上連接為一體，因此，人類並非天然擁有萬物，也不是萬物的中心，而是作為萬物之一與萬物平權。人類社會活動中的本能，也清晰體現出這一平權現象：個體對「不被擁有的獨立自主」狀態有著強烈的嚮往與執著。這不僅是心理需求，更是生命本體對「平權狀態」自然回歸的一種渴望，就如水流會嚮往低處。
人類對公平的認知差異始終存在，因需求、分配與認知差異引發的爭執從未間斷。儘管如此，人類追求公平的腳步從未停下，依然持續不懈地用血淚與汗水對公平進行賦義。然而，人類對公平的賦義，卻始終處於「跨本體擁有權」所確立的階級框架內，這導致平等概念一直都被統治階級所主導與定義，因此，平等一直都是統治階級的敘事工具，並且其生成的主流價值觀也只能是統治階級公平觀。
這些公平觀，均與「平權」這種天然平等狀態相去甚遠，現代人對自由的追求，本質上正在靠向平權狀態。然而，平權必須以「獨立自主」為成立前提，無法獨立，便意味著置身於控制之中，任何控制之下的自由，都是被允許的自由，其本身就是不自由，人們似乎還沒有充分意識到這一點。必須要說明的是，獨立自主並不必然妨礙個體之間「共跳同一支舞」。

「跨本體擁有權」作為先驗性的底層邏輯，已成為人類社會世代遺傳的文化基因，將人類文明緊密綑綁。它讓人類理所當然地認為：一塊土地、一頭牛、甚至一個人及未來，都「應該」屬於某個主體或某種價值。這種集體共識，導致人類長期混淆了物理真實中的擁有與符號宣稱中的擁有。人類文明，正是在「宣稱擁有」與「為公平賦義」的堆疊與交織中奮力前行。

A Narrative History of Human Society (II): Civilization Begun by Theocracy (Part I) — Fairness

God, whether real or imagined, attained a position of authority through His supernatural powers and status as creator. In human society, He acquired unquestioned legitimacy and became one of the most distinctive supreme power structures in human history.
Whether or not God truly exists, human offerings and sacrifices to God have undeniably existed throughout history. Even today, traces of the divine remain embedded in food traditions, marriage ethics, funeral rituals, and festival celebrations. The master-servant ownership structure in which “God is the master, humanity the servant, and all things the property of God” has become deeply rooted in human civilization and has continued through successive stages of social evolution.
God functions much like the most fundamental language through which humanity understands the world. Interwoven with everyday life, the idea of God permeates virtually every ideology. The special emotions generated by God's power, status, and role as creator flow through collective cooperation and daily existence like the gentle gaze of a mother. At the same time, dominance relationships and territorial instincts, which are widespread throughout the animal kingdom, enjoy broad psychological acceptance within human societies.
Consequently, it became natural to take God's ownership of all things as the template for human social relations. From this foundation emerged systems of master-servant authority organized through symbolic ownership, gradually evolving into patterns of social roles, obligations, and belonging. Bloodlines likewise gained legitimacy through association with divine prestige and spread widely throughout society, while historically distinctive class systems gradually took shape.
God's symbolic ownership of all things functioned much like the original coordinate system of human civilization, continuously determining both the direction and the development of civilizational progress.

Labor cooperation organized through ownership relations proved more efficient and more stable than loose forms of collaboration. Under the pressures of natural selection, decentralized cooperation was gradually replaced by systems of coordinated domination based upon ownership structures.
In eras of low productivity, master-servant relationships and systems of status and authority organized through symbolic ownership enabled societies to allocate people and resources with considerable efficiency. Such arrangements helped humanity gain advantages in the struggle for survival and ultimately rise to a dominant position in the natural world.
For individuals, however, this meant being compelled to accept assigned social roles within predetermined hierarchical positions. As a result, later generations often came to believe that one's social origins and destiny were innate and predetermined at birth.
Throughout the development of civilization, symbolic ownership gradually evolved into a stable and institutionalized form of social power. Through symbolic claims and social role performance, it enabled the social world to establish possession over physical entities without any direct physical incorporation between owner and owned.
Such ownership exists entirely within human consciousness. It differs fundamentally from bodily ownership, such as possessing one's own hands or feet. When the owner and the owned are separated in symbolic ownership, meaning and emotional attachment may disappear, yet no physical injury is inflicted upon either party.
Nevertheless, it is precisely this symbolic ownership—with its mental acts of boundary-drawing and meaning-assignment—that forms the basic structural framework of social organization.
Consider the statement: "This money is mine."
The money itself is not physically part of the owner's body, nor can it be controlled in the same manner as one's limbs. Yet within social relationships, the money is recognized as belonging to the entity represented by the symbol "I." What the individual actually utilizes is not merely the physical object, but the social meaning attached to it—its associated status, resources, opportunities, and networks of relationships.
To possess money is therefore not merely to possess an object; it is also to affirm one's social position, social connections, and accessible resources.
The same logic applies to statements such as "This land belongs to China." Through collectively recognized boundaries and socially assigned meanings, the symbol "China" acquires exclusive authority over a physical territory.
Likewise, when one says, "This is an American," the individual is symbolically incorporated into the social entity called America, protected and governed through that symbolic affiliation while simultaneously excluded from competing claims made by other political entities.
These examples reveal what may be considered one of the fundamental structural features of social reality: a social order organized around symbolic ownership.

In the physical world, entities may stand in relationships of coexistence, intersection, inclusion, or exclusion. Yet none of these relationships inherently constitute domination or subordination.
Even relationships often described as subordinate are fundamentally structural relations of belonging or containment rather than relations of command and obedience. In other words, no natural master-servant hierarchy exists among things themselves. Each entity operates independently according to its own nature.
This condition of mutual independence—where things neither dominate, possess, nor subordinate one another—may be called Pingquan, the equal standing of all things.
Pingquan is both a natural physical condition and a natural form of equality.
The relationship between human beings and their possessions, by contrast, exists only through symbolic constructions of consciousness. Human beings are therefore not naturally the owners of all things, nor are they the center of existence. Rather, they exist as one part among many within a state of Pingquan.
Human behavior itself reveals this tendency. Individuals display a persistent desire for independent existence free from ownership by others. This is not merely a psychological preference but a deeper inclination toward a return to Pingquan—a tendency as natural as water flowing downhill.
Human disagreements about fairness have always existed. Conflicts arising from differences in needs, distribution, and perception have never ceased.
Yet despite these disputes, humanity has never abandoned the pursuit of fairness. Across generations, people have continued to define, redefine, and struggle over its meaning through sacrifice, labor, and conflict.
The problem, however, is that these definitions of fairness have almost always been formulated within the hierarchical framework established by symbolic ownership. As a result, concepts of equality have historically been defined by ruling groups and deployed as narrative instruments of power. The dominant values of any era therefore tend to reflect the ruling class's conception of fairness.
These conceptions remain far removed from Pingquan as a natural condition of equality.
Modern aspirations toward freedom may, in many respects, be understood as movements toward Pingquan. Yet Pingquan requires independence as a precondition. Where independence is absent, control is present. Any freedom that exists only by permission remains a form of unfreedom.
This distinction remains insufficiently recognized.
At the same time, independence does not necessarily prevent individuals from participating in collective cooperation—from "dancing the same dance" together.

As a foundational and inherited logic, symbolic ownership has become one of the enduring cultural genes of human civilization.
It leads people to assume that land, livestock, resources, other persons, and even the future itself ought to belong to some owner, authority, or value system.
This collective assumption has caused humanity to conflate physical possession with symbolic claims of ownership for much of its history.
Human civilization has advanced through the continual intertwining of two processes: the declaration of ownership and the ongoing effort to define fairness.
It is within this intersection that the history of civilization has unfolded.




神權文明：公開的特權秩序

神因其絕對擁有權，在人類社會中具備至高處置權，成為獨一無二的終極裁量者，於人類而言，「人及萬物皆為神的資產」是理所當然的宇宙真理。因此，神對社會角色的不同安排，構成了世俗間的階級與地位，神對自己資產的不同擺放，則構成了人間的貧富與苦樂。
在此框架下，無論地位高低、財富多少，皆為神的僕役，公平，被定義為「神之下」的絕對趨同——當每個人都是神的僕人時，人與人之間便達成了平等；當每個人都遵循神所安排的階級秩序時，社會便被視為公平。至此，每一種存在都被納入神的擁有秩序，成為神權體係的組織部分，在確定的座標位置上各守其位、各盡其職，共同履行對神的義務、祈求神的恩典、延續神的榮耀。
在這樣的公平原則中，特權階級因神的地位安排得以正當擁有奴隸，奴役驅使也是正當與公開的履行神職。這種由擁有秩序所主導的主從關係，構成了人類社會影響深遠的權力模型，這一理解世界的方式，深深烙印在人類集體意識共識場中，成為後世社會權力正當性的源頭。
例如，君王統治成立於「君權神授」這一共識性前提，其「擁有與驅使人民」的權力，因獲得集體認可而具備絕對正當性；貴族因血統神化這一共識性前提而享有奴隸與資源；平民因普遍接受「平民須遵循神的佈局而勞作與供奉」這一共通認知，勞作與供奉便成為平民的宿命；奴隸更是因其將服從與犧牲內化為自身的義務與榮耀，才自主獻祭。​
這一初始化的公平秩序，使人們從一開始就習慣於接受「有人統治、有人服從、有人指揮驅使、有人履行義務、還得有人當牛有人做馬」的設定並形成集體共識。

在這樣的公平觀中，所有人都以自身處於神意安排的位置，作為存在正當性，維護神意設定的角色地位與主從秩序，便成為僕人的本份。也就是說，統治者貴族與平民奴隸共存同一舞臺的這一階級現象本身就是公平，而任何角色越位、破壞階级關係的行為，都是對公平的褻瀆與破壞。人們深信，凡納入神的擁有秩序者，都將在神的注視中獲得保護與安頓——神之擁有秩序的穩定，正是社會秩序的穩定。
這一將服從定義為義務、將階級視為公平的秩序中，不允許人以自身作為權利起點，被肆意指揮驅使也不是壓迫，而是秩序與榮耀。因此，社會中既不存在為個體權利辯護的邏輯，也不存在被驅使者的「受害者身份」，特權階級更無需為其財富與地位優勢做出解釋。而任何關注個體權利者、任何階級地位的思考者、任何不願被驅使者，都是對神不敬者，不僅是信仰的破壞者，更是社會秩序的顛覆者。
神之下的公平秩序，使貴族與統治階級獲得了擁有奴隸、財產、土地乃至國家的正當性，也使他們進一步獲得了建立王朝、統治社會的正當性。在社會資源有限的條件下，統治者為維護自身統治而在彼此間爆發的紛爭，充斥著整個人類文明發展史。相較之下，被安排為平民與奴隸者，則是經歷了無數代人的抗爭，才逐漸取得這項「跨本體擁有權」，得以正當擁有自己的財物。尤其在工業革命後生產力大幅提高的近代，平權運動的崛起，促使專屬於特權階級的跨本體擁有權，演變成為普通人的根本權力，正如當今人們將「財產（跨本體擁有物）神聖不可侵犯」奉為現代社會的核心原則。

​行至今日，昔日「神擁有萬物」的神聖正當性，與民眾長期抗爭後取得的「私有產權正當性」，共同轉化為現代人對「佔有萬物」的理所當然。人類集體產生了一種幻覺，以為自身已從「神的僕役」，進化成了世界中心與萬物的絕對支配者。


Theocratic Civilization: An Open Order of Privilege
By virtue of His absolute ownership, God possesses supreme authority over disposition and judgment within human society, standing as the singular and ultimate arbiter of all things. For humanity, the proposition that "human beings and all things are God's property" is regarded as a self-evident cosmic truth.
Accordingly, God's different arrangements of social roles establish distinctions of rank and status in the secular world, while His different placements of His own possessions determine wealth and poverty, joy and suffering among human beings.
Within this framework, regardless of status or wealth, all people are servants of God. Fairness is defined as absolute conformity under God: when everyone is equally God's servant, equality among human beings is achieved; when everyone follows the hierarchical order ordained by God, society is regarded as fair.
Thus, every form of existence becomes incorporated into God's ownership order as an organizational component of the theocratic system. Each occupies an assigned position, fulfills a designated role, performs obligations toward God, seeks divine grace, and contributes to the continuation of divine glory.
Within this conception of fairness, privileged classes are regarded as legitimately entitled to own slaves by virtue of the positions assigned to them by God. The domination and command of others are likewise understood as proper and openly acknowledged fulfillments of divine duty.
This master-servant order, structured through ownership relations, became one of the most influential models of power in human history. This way of understanding the world became deeply embedded within humanity's collective consciousness and later served as a primary source of legitimacy for social and political authority.
For example, the authority of kings rested upon the shared premise of the Divine Right of Kings. Their power to own and command the people acquired absolute legitimacy through collective recognition. Nobles possessed slaves and resources because their bloodlines were sanctified. Commoners accepted labor and tribute as their destined role because they internalized the belief that ordinary people must work and make offerings according to God's design. Even slaves came to regard obedience and sacrifice as duty and honor, willingly participating in their own subordination.
This initial conception of fairness accustomed people from the very beginning to accept a social order in which some rule, some obey, some command, some serve, and some labor like beasts of burden. Over time, these assumptions solidified into collective consensus.

Within this conception of fairness, every individual derives the legitimacy of existence from the position assigned by divine will. Maintaining the roles, statuses, and master-servant order established by God becomes the proper duty of every servant.
In other words, the very existence of a hierarchical society—one in which rulers and nobles coexist alongside commoners and slaves—is itself regarded as fairness. Any attempt to step beyond one's assigned role or disrupt class relations is viewed as a desecration and destruction of fairness.
People firmly believed that all who were incorporated into God's ownership order would receive protection and proper placement under divine watch. The stability of God's ownership order was therefore equated with the stability of society itself.
Within a system that defines obedience as duty and hierarchy as fairness, individuals are not permitted to regard themselves as the starting point of rights. Being arbitrarily commanded or driven by others is not considered oppression, but rather order and honor.
Consequently, there exists neither a logic for defending individual rights nor a concept of victimhood for those who are commanded. Privileged classes have no need to justify their wealth or superior status. Anyone concerned with individual rights, anyone who questions social hierarchy, or anyone unwilling to be commanded is viewed as disrespectful toward God—not merely a destroyer of faith, but a threat to the foundations of social order itself.
The fair order established under God granted nobles and ruling classes the legitimate right to own slaves, property, land, and even entire states. It further granted them legitimacy to establish dynasties and govern societies.
Under conditions of limited resources, conflicts among rulers seeking to preserve and expand their dominance came to fill the pages of human history.
By contrast, those assigned the roles of commoners and slaves endured countless generations of struggle before gradually acquiring this same capacity for symbolic ownership and thereby obtaining the legitimate right to own property themselves.
Particularly after the Industrial Revolution dramatically expanded productive capacity, the rise of Pingquan movements transformed symbolic ownership—once reserved for privileged classes—into a fundamental right of ordinary people.
This transformation is reflected in the modern principle that property, as an object of symbolic ownership, is sacred and inviolable.

Today, the sacred legitimacy once embodied in the idea that "God owns all things" has merged with the legitimacy of private property acquired through centuries of popular struggle.
Together they have evolved into a modern conviction: that possessing and claiming ownership of things is self-evidently justified.
Humanity has consequently fallen into a collective illusion—the belief that it has evolved from being servants of God into becoming the center of the world and the absolute master of all existence.


宗教文明：內化的靈魂約束

當人類面臨巨大的生存壓力時，特權階級的存在正當性，終究無法抹除人們被奴役所產生的不公感，在與暴力統治的對抗以及對生存的渴求中，生命體對平權的渴望逐漸甦醒，恐懼與死亡匯聚於對神靈恩澤的嚮往，整合了神權機制並為人生賦予意義的宗教，便應時而生。
無論是創世神的降臨、神明的轉世，抑或是人類覺悟者的開示，宗教的創立，借用了人類對「神」或「天」極致而特別的底層情感，調動了人類對未知的恐懼、對死亡的焦慮、對公平的渴望、對終極意義的追尋，以及對無限、完美且強大存在的絕對依賴。
宗教往往以家庭關係作為核心隱喻，調動人類的從眾本能組織大規模的集體趨同、共舞儀式，使信眾獲得心理慰籍與靈魂歸屬感；這讓人感受到「活著不僅僅是為了生存，更是在踐行宏大而神聖的目標」，這種意義感是人類精神世界中不可或缺的組成部分，也是宗教在物質生活已大幅改善的今天，依然頑強存活的本質原因。
宗教所描繪的輪迴、天堂地獄、涅槃、極樂世界、脫離苦海乃至「大庇天下寒士俱歡顏」等公平願景，本質上都是在解釋痛苦、救贖靈魂、生成超現實的平等預期或對未來公平的許諾，滿足信眾對幸福生活的嚮往。
這些趨同共舞與心靈構架，在愛與恐懼的交織、因果與罪咎的流淌、期待與敬畏的約束中，讓渡了個體的獨立性，共同投入「母親般溫暖的懷抱」——這，便是信仰。
當信仰成為信眾內心最親近的不可拒絕者，信眾便將自己的思想和行為交付給信仰，如手腳服從頭腦那樣，遵循信仰的指引，在無拒絕的自主思考中，形成自我統治。

宗教的組織形態，既有高度中心化、等級森嚴的「金字塔」結構，也有如新教、印度教這樣的去中心化網絡，還有中國這樣，以「天」為核心信仰，具有宗教的所有功能、回答了宗教的終極問題，卻無明確宗教實體的獨特形態。中國文化包容的根本含義，恰恰在於這種「無實體狀態下的收為己有」，其「家天下」理念，正是「天道」秩序的世俗化投影。
在歷史演化中，具有「信仰代理人」的宗教，因潛在的「信仰擁有信眾」集體共識，代理人得以攜信仰之名，獲得擁有權威，控制「孩子們」的思想與生活的方方面面，這使得信仰體系與世俗統治高度重疊，實現了信眾自主配合統治的完美協同。在無明確代理人的宗教，因「自己的信仰是唯一真理」的排他邏輯，一旦不同敘事系統在同一物理空間中交織，相互催生的仇怨、矛盾與衝突便不可避免。
宗教團體的「規則內一致性」，構築成信息繭房，形成了對內高度自洽卻必須排外的邏輯閉環。因此，即便是現代最開明的宗教信眾，宣稱尊重良知自由與多元權利、拒絕迫害與報復，其思維本質仍是以「信仰」為不可侵犯的至高權威，以繭房內邏輯理解社會。這種閉環性與唯一性，其本身便在沖擊自由與多元。宗教在人類文明中寫下了濃墨重彩的一筆，而宗教衝突，則是畫出了濃墨重彩的百筆千筆。

越是內部自洽的敘事系統，其結構越是趨於穩固，也越是需要建構一堵更高的高牆將內外分割。不僅宗教如此，不同文明系統、不同價值敘事、不同公平標準，無論它們是意識形態、政治立場，還是民族、國家，甚至是現代社交媒體圈層，其繭房邏輯都在建構心靈高牆，而圍繞「互相拆牆」所造成的文化碰撞與衝突從未間斷。
究其本質，皆是在保護自身內部結構自洽的同時，既妄想「同化並融合對方」，又極力「拒絕被對方融入」。融合對方與被對方融入本是同一邏輯，因此，融合對方的同時卻拒絕被對方融入，正是自我矛盾的邏輯妄想。這種跨系統接觸產生的結構性排異，除非一方結構受到破壞或重塑，否則不可調和，因而彼此表面上的「承認與尊重」，必然是暫時的虛與委蛇。宗教作為高度一致性的信仰群體，結構變動即意味著分裂或重建。

當代地緣衝突包括地緣政治衝突中， 宗教敘事、民族敘事、國家敘事、世俗公平敘事正在重疊碰撞，原本無關的每一個體，都因被它們擁有而被迫裹挾進紛爭的洪流之中。
從神權文明開始的特權邏輯，僅僅更換了語言與形式，卻始終以同一套「主僕擁有關係」，在不同時代的文明敘事中頑強地宣稱與反覆重寫自身。



Religious Civilization: The Internalized Discipline of the Soul

Religious Civilization: Internalized Constraint of the Soul
When humanity faces extreme survival pressure, the legitimacy of privileged classes cannot fully erase the sense of injustice produced by being dominated. In the struggle against violent rule and under the imperative of survival, the organism’s latent desire for equality gradually awakens. Fear and death converge into a longing for divine grace. In this process, religion emerges as a system that integrates mechanisms of divine rule while simultaneously endowing human life with meaning.
Whether in the form of the descent of a creator deity, the reincarnation of gods, or the enlightenment of human sages, the formation of religion draws upon humanity’s most fundamental emotional layer toward “God” or “Heaven.” It mobilizes fear of the unknown, anxiety over death, desire for fairness, the search for ultimate meaning, and an absolute dependence on an infinite, perfect, and omnipotent existence.
Religion often uses familial relationships as its core metaphor. Through this, it activates human herd instincts and organizes large-scale collective synchronization and ritualized “dancing together,” allowing believers to obtain psychological comfort and a sense of spiritual belonging. It produces the feeling that “life is not merely for survival, but for the enactment of a grand and sacred purpose.” This sense of meaning is an indispensable component of the human spiritual world and remains the fundamental reason religion persists even in modern societies where material conditions have vastly improved.
Religious narratives—such as reincarnation, heaven and hell, nirvana, the pure land, liberation from suffering, or even utopian visions like “all the world’s poor share in joy”—are fundamentally frameworks for interpreting suffering, redeeming the soul, and constructing transcendental expectations of equality or promises of future justice. They satisfy humanity’s longing for happiness and completeness.
These structures of synchronized ritual and mental construction, interwoven with love and fear, karma and guilt, anticipation and reverence, gradually relinquish individual autonomy and immerse individuals into a “maternal embrace.” This is what we call faith.
When faith becomes the closest and least rejectable force within the believer’s inner world, individuals hand over their thoughts and actions to it. Like limbs obeying the brain, they follow its guidance and form a state of self-governance without explicit coercion or conscious refusal.
Religions take multiple organizational forms: highly centralized hierarchical pyramids, decentralized networks such as certain branches of Protestantism or Hindu traditions, and even unique systems such as China’s, which centers on “Heaven” (Tian) as a core principle. This form contains all functional properties of religion and answers ultimate existential questions, yet lacks a clearly defined institutional body. The essence of Chinese cultural inclusiveness lies precisely in this ability to “incorporate without physical embodiment.” Its notion of “family-state order” is a secular projection of the “Heavenly order.”
Historically, religions with designated “faith intermediaries” operate under a latent collective consensus of “symbolic ownership of believers by faith.” Through this, intermediaries acquire authority in the name of faith and gain control over the thoughts and lives of the “children.” This produces a deep overlap between religious systems and secular governance, achieving seamless coordination in which believers voluntarily align themselves with systems of control.
In religions without clear intermediaries, the logic that “my faith is the only truth” produces an exclusivity principle. When different narrative systems coexist within the same physical space, the mutual generation of hostility, contradiction, and conflict becomes inevitable.
The internal consistency of religious organizations constructs informational enclaves—closed logical loops that are highly coherent internally yet structurally exclusionary externally. Thus, even the most liberal modern believers, while professing respect for conscience, freedom, diversity, and opposition to persecution, still operate under a cognitive structure in which faith is the supreme and non-negotiable authority, interpreting society through an enclosed logic. This closure and exclusivity inherently challenge pluralism and freedom. Religion has written a deeply influential chapter in human civilization, and religious conflict has written not just pages, but volumes upon volumes.
The more internally coherent a narrative system becomes, the more stable its structure, and the more it requires walls to separate inside from outside. This applies not only to religion, but also to ideological systems, political positions, national identities, and even modern social-media-based subcultures. All such enclave logics construct mental walls, and cultural collisions arising from attempts to “break walls” have never ceased.
At their core, these systems simultaneously attempt to assimilate others while refusing to be assimilated themselves. Yet assimilation and being assimilated are structurally identical processes. Therefore, the desire to assimilate others while rejecting being assimilated is a fundamental logical contradiction. The structural rejection produced by cross-system contact cannot be resolved unless one side undergoes collapse or reconstruction. Thus, any apparent mutual recognition or respect is only temporary and strategic.
In contemporary geopolitical conflicts, religious narratives, ethnic narratives, national narratives, and secular justice narratives overlap and collide. Individuals, once unrelated, become engulfed in these torrents simply by being “owned” by these systems of meaning.
Since the beginning of divine civilization, the logic of privileged order has never disappeared. It has merely changed language and form, while continuously reproducing the same underlying structure: a master–servant order grounded in symbolic ownership, repeatedly rewritten across different historical epochs of civilization.

現代文明：隱匿的特權秩序

現代文明建構了以個體權利為核心的法律秩序，將每個人都納入這一法治框架之中。它透過人權、契約、自由等嶄新敘事，勾勒出一個充滿希望的藍圖：每個人皆享有與生俱來的基本權利，每個人都能受到平等對待。
這種公平觀強調個體在機會與規則面前的平等，重視多元與包容。它試圖正視個體處境的差異，將個人從傳統的主僕秩序中解放出來；它致力於讓每個人都能生活在有尊嚴的社會環境中，享有公平的社會保障與平等的發展機會。這一公平觀已成為現代社會穩定與繁榮的基石，並正在靠向「平權」這一天然、獨立且自主的平等狀態。
無論是神權、王權還是宗教制度，其各自的公平價值觀都曾在歷史上推動過社會發展與文明進步。現代公平系統，透過對與生俱來基本權利的追求，確立了個體私有財產的法律保障，賦予普羅大眾擁有私人財產的神聖正當性。這使得原本僅屬於特權階級的「跨本體擁有權」，成為人人都可具備的普世權力，為個體注入了極致強大的驅動力，在擁有秩序所提供的高效協同之下，人類僅用百年時間便超越了過去千年，締造了以人為中心的文明盛世。

然而，跨本體擁有關係，是以擁有者為核心的中心裁定秩序，無服從者的裁定並無意義，因而裁定秩序必須依賴服從者的存在而存在。這意味著，跨本體擁有關係必然生成「有人統治、有人服從」的階級結構，以個體權利為主導的這套現代公平系統，便必然在階級秩序中運行。從而，社會無論如何繁榮興盛，制度設計無論如何精巧，法律條文無論如何繁複完善，階級意志都不能被消除，階級歧視始終存在，階級壓迫無法停止。
現實正是如此，個體權利意志與階級支配意志——結構上對立排斥的兩種意識型態，荒謬地共存於現代公平所倡導的多元與包容之中。為維持這一荒謬共存的持續與穩定，其代價便是持續製造新的荒誕。例如，為實現平等而建立新的差別對待；為反對歧視而生成新的身份區分；為保障某些群體的表達權利而限制另一些群體的表達權利；為維護多元而排斥非主流的多元價值觀。於是，大學能以「多元包容」之名反向限制特定學生的團體活動；企業能以提高少數族裔比例的理由而形成新的種族篩選機制；社群平台與校園也能以保護特定群體不受冒犯為藉口，限制封殺部分政治或宗教觀點的表達。
在消除階級的道路上，新的階級正在被持續生產；在追求平等的過程中，新的不平等也正在被不斷創造。現代公平試圖擺脫特權，靈魂卻與特權的生成邏輯並肩同行。

神權時代，「你必須服從我」是公開的理所當然；在現代文明中，「我包容你」則是公開的理所當然。在本質上，「我包容你」是將「你必須服從我」變身為「我允許你與我不同」，隱匿了「允許」本身的地位宣告。換言之，「我包容你」實質上是隱匿中的上位者對下位者的施捨，然而卻荒謬地獲得普世贊美且不容置疑更不能被批評。
更荒誕的現實是，身處階級秩序之中的被包容者，其所感受到的往往並非尊重與公平，而是只能等待「被裁定、被接納」的壓迫、屈辱與無奈。歷史上處於下位者的心理，在被迫處境中生成的，往往是反噬上位者的快感，英國「誘拐幫派」事件，正是這種包容的荒誕性所反映出的、不同群體之間長期積累的不信任與深層敵意。
階級意志中的包容不是平等，只是階級秩序中的一種緩和技術。這種暫時的心理撫慰不僅無法消除階級雙方內心深處的敵意，反而在隱性矛盾的不斷堆疊中，持續強化彼此的割裂與對立，正如歐美社會正在經歷的。
以身份背景決定施與對象，卻不能一視同仁的善意，並不能稱為同情心，而是以身份地位為依託的階級立場。薩阿得在《致命的同情心》中所揭示的，正是這種選擇性的善意與同情——其本質是依據身份標籤進行的階級裁定，並非源於同情心本身。現實中的荒誕，也並非產生於包容，而是產生於包容背後所隱匿的階級立場與特權意志。
真正的平等關係，從不需要任何一方去包容另一方。「我能夠包容」從來都不是「我需要包容」的理由。彼此獨立的存在，原本並不需等待被誰寬容與接納。
現代社會結構，已從「神中心化」的裁定結構，轉化為「有法可依與有人服從」的法律裁定結構。法律裁定顯現為個體依照自身自由意志，依法享有的自由、尊重、多元、公平與包容的權利。然而「依法」本身便隱藏了其背後的上位者立場——法律制度的設定權、公平的定義權，以及話語解釋權，始終在主導者、裁定者的控制中。
神權時代的階級特權是具象且公開的，而在多元、包容與平等旗幟下運轉的現代文明，階級觀念已與財產擁有、國家擁有、身份擁有、資源壟斷深度交融，並隱形於契約、財產、法律、身份以及一切現代社會角色與意識形態之中。「誰可以被允許、誰應當被排除」的人為裁定權從未消失，它只是從曾經可見的皮鞭，轉化為現代文明中無實體而無可指認，卻又無處不在的幽靈。

從「神擁有萬物」伊始，伴隨文明進程，跨本體擁有權的主體在不斷變換，但意識想要擁有更多的慾望卻從未改變，現實中的資源也總是確定而有限，擁有物無論如何也不可能人人一致。因此，只要允許「跨本體擁有」存在，就必然生成身份、地位、財富上的差距及差異性需求，也就是階層觀念、身份政治都將被持續醞釀與滋養。當差異性需求被制度化保護，而社會依舊建立在階級身份的土壤之上時，差異保護便會逐漸演化為結構性特權競爭。於是，「包容差異」不再是平等關係中的共存與協調，而是逐漸轉變為不同利益群體對公共資源、規則制度與文化空間的重新掠食。這也註定了「實現人人平等的同時又能充分尊重差異」，不過是另一場類似共產主義的烏托邦。
從亞當·斯密到哈耶克，從凱恩斯到弗里德曼，在政府對市場監管的邊界上，思想家們進行過長期辯論。然而，這些充滿智慧的爭論，因受困於跨本體擁有權所確立的階級秩序框架，便只能是在統治意志之下、依據統治者需求所劃定的市場監管邊界。
萬物在獨立自主中的自由互動，是物理事實，「自由市場」是這一物理真實延伸至社會世界的表現；而在階級系統中，「監管」則是社會秩序維持與存續的必然選擇，它們都無法消除而必須共存。因此，監管與自由市場只能伴隨人類對公平的不同詮釋，在現實中相互拉扯、妥協、此消彼長。當今崇尚自由化價值的美國，在監管與市場化的拉扯中，靠向了市場化的邊緣，籍此引領了現代科技與金融的繁榮；而歐盟則走向強監管路徑，鎖死了系統內部的創新活力，擴張了其在世界舞臺上規則制定的權力邊界。
同樣作為當代秩序的締造者與維護者，因對公平秩序的不同解讀，便以不同方式書寫了市場與監管的邊界。顯然，決定邊界的既不是技術、經濟，也不是制度本身，而是意識形態，是「階級控制意志」與「天然獨立意志」之間的邊界。同樣，無論是共產主義者還是反共產主義者，他們所嚮往的美好未來，本質上都是人為設計的世俗極樂世界，這無可厚非。但由於擁有秩序中必然滋生的階級意志，與天然的獨立意志無法共存，導致一切擁有秩序下的極樂世界只能淪為烏托邦，甚至演變成人間地獄。

現代公平觀下的文明制度，成功地將暴力與心靈控制融入體制的日常運行之中，演化出一臺強大的階級隱匿機器。這臺機器透過資源知識的壟斷、算法的規訓與話語權操縱，正在將現實的荒謬正當化，並將階級改頭換面為無實體不可見的「邏輯存在」，無論人們是否承認，財物掠奪正以國家、財團與市場的合法競爭呈現，奴役藏匿於雇佣、生產與契約之中，越來越多無可指認的特權，也正在制度授權下合法完成更大規模的佔有，獲貪腐之實卻無貪腐之名。
個體在或為生存掙扎、或為生活奔波的同時，直面的是冰冷的算法、繁複的條文與被針對的無邊推薦，或者在迷茫的虛無或者在醉生夢死也或者在邊緣化中無路可逃。
系統結構本身的危機，任何個體都無法單獨對抗，特權們也不會例外。在錯綜複雜的全球一體化網絡中，誰是誰的特權階級已交織纏繞、無從分割，這導致特權階級自身也存在虛無般的焦慮。每一個人都找不到受力點的虛無狀態，正是人類陷入集體焦慮的物理本質。即便如此，特權的便利性只會使其繼續強大，資源也仍會加速集中。現實中，極少數團體已掌握全球絕大部分財富，並且還在繼續形成更大規模的佔有。根據World Inequality Report 2026，頂層10%擁有全球財富的75%，底層一半人類僅擁有2%；頂層0.001%擁有的財富約為底層一半人類的3倍，其財富份額從1995年的近4%上升到目前的6%以上，且增長速度遠高於底層。

從神開始，擁有者一直在改變，但是擁有他人他物的邏輯從未改變，神明、君王與貴族因跨本體擁有而成就了屬於他們的公平時代；在近代，越來越多的人，因獲得了擁有財物的神聖正當性，締造了普通人的公平時代。公平觀確定了人類文明的前進方向，而跨本體擁有權則確定了公平觀的確立方向，人類面臨的問題從來不是公平本身，而是公平究竟以什麼為基礎。
在普通人因「擁有」而喜上眉梢之際，捍衛既有擁有與獲取更多擁有之間的衝突，便悄然來臨。在科技不斷放大人類改造世界能力的同時，跨本體擁有權樹立的擁有野望，終將親手摧毀其一手築就的文明秩序。

Modern Civilization: An Invisible Order of Privilege
Modern civilization constructs a legal order centered on individual rights, incorporating every person into a unified framework of law. Through narratives such as human rights, contracts, and freedom, it sketches an apparently hopeful blueprint: every individual possesses inherent fundamental rights and is entitled to equal treatment.
This conception of fairness emphasizes equality before opportunities and rules, and values diversity and inclusion. It seeks to acknowledge differences in individual conditions and to liberate people from the traditional master–servant order. It aims to ensure that every person can live in a society with dignity, enjoying fair social protection and equal opportunities for development. This notion of fairness has become the foundation of modern stability and prosperity, and is gradually approaching a natural, independent, and self-sustaining state of equality.
Whether in theocratic systems, monarchies, or religious institutions, each historical conception of fairness has contributed to social development and civilizational progress. The modern system of fairness, through the pursuit of inherent rights, establishes legal protection of private property and grants ordinary individuals the sacred legitimacy of ownership. This transforms what was once the exclusive privilege of elites into a universal capacity. It injects powerful individual agency into society, and under the efficiency of an ownership-based coordination system, humanity in just a century surpassed the progress of the previous millennium, creating a civilization centered on the individual.
However, symbolic ownership is a center-based adjudicative order grounded in the owner as the reference point. Without those who are subject to adjudication, adjudication itself becomes meaningless; thus, such an order necessarily depends on the existence of those who are subjected to it. This implies that symbolic ownership inevitably generates a hierarchical structure of “those who govern and those who obey.” A modern system of fairness centered on individual rights therefore still operates within a hierarchical order. No matter how prosperous society becomes, how refined institutions are designed, or how complex legal systems grow, class-based will cannot be eliminated, and class discrimination and structural domination persist.
Reality reflects this clearly: the will of individual rights and the will of hierarchical control—two ideologically opposed structures—coexist paradoxically within the modern discourse of diversity and inclusion. To sustain this paradoxical coexistence, society continuously produces new forms of contradiction. In the name of equality, new forms of differentiation are created; in the name of anti-discrimination, new identity categories emerge; in order to protect the expressive rights of some groups, the expression of others is restricted; in the name of diversity, non-mainstream values are excluded.
Thus universities may restrict certain student organizations in the name of “diversity and inclusion”; corporations may implement new forms of selection justified by increasing minority representation; and social media platforms or campuses may suppress certain political or religious expressions under the pretext of protecting vulnerable groups from offense. On the path toward eliminating hierarchy, new hierarchies are continuously produced; in the pursuit of equality, new inequalities are continuously generated. Modern fairness attempts to escape privilege, yet its underlying logic walks in parallel with the reproduction of privilege itself.
In the age of theocracy, “you must obey me” was openly self-evident. In modern civilization, “I include you” has become equally self-evident. In essence, “I include you” is a transformation of “you must obey me” into “I allow you to be different from me,” thereby concealing the very act of authorization. In other words, “inclusion” is often a subtle form of hierarchical concession by the superior to the subordinate, yet it is paradoxically celebrated as universal virtue and placed beyond criticism.
Even more paradoxically, those who are “included” often do not experience respect or equality, but instead a state of waiting—to be evaluated, accepted, or rejected—producing feelings of pressure, humiliation, and helplessness. Historically, such asymmetry often generates retaliatory psychology among the subordinated. Events such as Britain’s “grooming gangs” have been interpreted as manifestations of long-term mistrust and deep-seated hostility produced under such structures of conditional inclusion.
In this sense, inclusion within hierarchical logic is not equality, but a mechanism of stabilization within an unequal order. This temporary psychological cushioning does not eliminate latent hostility between groups; instead, it reinforces division through accumulated structural tension, as seen in many contemporary Western societies. Selective benevolence—decided according to identity rather than universality—is not genuine compassion, but a form of class-based positioning. What Sahar Sadegh has described in Fatal Compassion reveals precisely this: compassion structured by identity categories is not compassion itself, but a disguised form of hierarchical adjudication.
True equality requires no party to “include” another. The claim “I am able to include you” is never a justification for “you need to be included.” Independent existence does not require permission to be accepted or tolerated.
Modern society has shifted from a centralized adjudicative structure of divine authority to a legal adjudicative structure of “rule of law.” Legal adjudication appears as the free exercise of individual will under rights such as freedom, respect, diversity, equality, and inclusion. Yet the principle of “lawfulness” itself conceals the position of the higher authority behind it: the power to define law, to define fairness, and to interpret meaning remains in the hands of those who design and control the system.
In theocratic civilization, class privilege was visible and explicit. In modern civilization operating under the banners of diversity, inclusion, and equality, class structure has become deeply interwoven with property ownership, national sovereignty, identity, and resource monopolies, and is embedded invisibly within contracts, law, identity systems, and ideological frameworks. The power to decide “who is allowed and who must be excluded” has not disappeared—it has merely transformed from a visible whip into an invisible, untraceable, yet omnipresent ghost.
From the initial condition of “God owns all things,” the subjects of symbolic ownership have continuously changed throughout civilizational development. Yet the desire to possess more has never changed. Resources in reality are finite and unevenly distributed; perfect equality of ownership is structurally impossible. Therefore, as long as symbolic ownership exists, differences in status, wealth, and hierarchy will inevitably emerge. These differences are continuously protected and institutionalized, giving rise to identity politics and class structure.
Once difference is protected within a system still grounded in hierarchical ownership relations, such protection evolves into structural competition over privilege. Thus, “inclusion of difference” is no longer coexistence or coordination among equals, but becomes a competition for resources, institutional influence, and cultural space among groups. The aspiration to achieve universal equality while fully preserving difference ultimately resembles another form of utopian projection.
From Adam Smith to Hayek, from Keynes to Friedman, intellectual debates over the boundary between market freedom and government regulation have continued for centuries. Yet these debates remain constrained within the framework of symbolic ownership and hierarchical order, and therefore ultimately reflect boundaries drawn under the demands of ruling structures.
The free interaction of independently existing entities is a physical reality; the “free market” is its social extension. Regulation, on the other hand, is a structural necessity for maintaining social order. Both must coexist and cannot be eliminated. Therefore, markets and regulation continuously interact, conflict, and balance each other through shifting interpretations of fairness. The United States, leaning toward marketization, has driven technological and financial expansion; the European Union, leaning toward regulation, has constrained internal innovation while expanding its power as a rule-making system.
Ultimately, the boundary between market and regulation is not determined by technology, economics, or institutional design, but by ideology—the boundary between hierarchical control will and natural independent existence. Similarly, whether one identifies as communist or anti-communist, the envisioned future is always a constructed secular utopia. Yet due to the unavoidable emergence of hierarchical will within systems of ownership, and its incompatibility with natural independence, all such utopias risk collapsing into either impossibility or systemic distortion.
Modern systems of fairness have successfully integrated violence and psychological control into everyday institutional operation, forming a powerful machine of hidden hierarchy. Through monopolies of knowledge and resources, algorithmic discipline, and control over discourse, this machine normalizes systemic absurdity and converts hierarchy into an invisible “logical entity.” Whether acknowledged or not, appropriation of wealth increasingly occurs through states, corporations, and market competition; forms of domination are concealed within employment, production, and contractual relations.
Individuals, while struggling for survival or livelihood, confront cold algorithmic systems, dense legal frameworks, and opaque recommendation systems. In a globally interconnected structure, systemic crisis cannot be resolved by any single individual. Even privileged groups are not exempt. In this entangled network, the identities of “who is privileged over whom” are so interwoven that even privilege itself becomes unstable and anxious. Yet the convenience of privilege ensures its continued expansion, and wealth continues to concentrate.
According to the World Inequality Report 2026, the top 10% hold 75% of global wealth, while the bottom half holds only 2%. The top 0.001% possess roughly three times the wealth of the bottom half of humanity, with their share increasing from nearly 4% in 1995 to over 6% today, growing significantly faster than the rest.
From the beginning of civilization, possessors have changed, but the logic of possessing others and things has not. Gods, kings, and aristocrats established their eras of fairness through symbolic ownership; in modern times, broader populations gained the legitimacy of ownership over property, forming a new era of “fairness for the many.” Fairness determines the direction of civilization, while symbolic ownership determines the direction of fairness itself. The core question has never been fairness itself, but the foundation upon which fairness is built.
As ordinary people rejoice in their ability to “own,” the conflict between preserving existing ownership and acquiring more ownership quietly emerges. As technological power amplifies humanity’s capacity to reshape the world, the ambition embedded in symbolic ownership may ultimately become the force that destabilizes the very civilization it helped construct.
